The shift

Banks rely on many tools to establish identity. None of them proves the person.

Identity, assumed

Device-native biometrics have been treated as proof of identity, but identity was only ever assumed: a PIN unlocks the phone just as well. Banking trojans now exploit that gap remotely. Zimperium has documented one targeting 349 banking, financial, e-wallet and cryptocurrency apps.

Fraud, and who pays for it

Account takeover, authorised push payment fraud and deepfake social engineering target every channel, bringing chargebacks, losses and the cost of insuring card portfolios. Under EU and UK rules, when a customer disputes a payment, the bank must prove it was authenticated.

Too many tools, too much exposure

Passwords, SMS codes, app pushes and tokens each add friction and another place where customer data must be protected. Each proves a fragment; together they still cannot prove who approved what.
